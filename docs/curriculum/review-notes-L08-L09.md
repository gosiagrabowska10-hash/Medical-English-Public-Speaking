**The practical skill each lesson builds**

- **L08:** projecting the voice with breath support instead of throat effort; microphone technique (including "turn and look, turn back, then speak"); using intonation for meaning (falls on findings, rise–rise–fall in lists, fall–rise for a reservation); purposeful variation of pitch, volume and pace; a five-step, two-minute voice warm-up; and voice care on long speaking days.
- **L09:** stance and the first five seconds; sectioned eye contact in a room and looking into the lens online; a small set of meaningful gestures (enumerating, contrasting, timeline, directing attention) and stillness for the key message; managing lectern, screen, pointer and clicker; and presence in online and hybrid sessions.

**Evidence and accuracy**

- **L08** cites research linking measured pitch variation to listeners' perception of liveliness in second-language presentations [Hincks, 2005], and survey evidence that voice disorders are reported more often by teachers than by the general population [Roy et al., 2004]. Breathing, projection and microphone technique are presented as **professional recommendations**. Persistent hoarseness is described as a clinical matter outside the scope of the programme.
- **L09** corrects two widespread myths, with sources: the misapplied "7–38–55 / 93% nonverbal" rule [Mehrabian & Ferris, 1967], and hormonal "power posing" claims, which were not replicated in a large study [Ranehill et al., 2015]. It cites research on rapid impressions from nonverbal behaviour [Ambady & Rosenthal, 1993] and a meta-analysis on gesture and comprehension [Hostetter, 2011]. It also notes that norms for eye contact and gesture vary between cultures.
- **Accent:** both lessons state explicitly that accent is not the target; the aim is intelligibility and authority.

**Pronunciation notation.** These lessons mark intonation with arrows (↘ falling, ↗ rising) rather than IPA, because their focus is melody rather than individual sounds. IPA is used where it adds value (see L07 and the W1 term card).

**Points I would particularly like your view on**

1. **Intonation guidance.** L08 describes rising intonation on statements as something that "can be heard by many listeners as uncertainty", while noting that it is common in some varieties of English. Is this balance right for an international audience?
2. **Cultural variation.** L09 recommends a "moderate and readable" style for eye contact and gesture at international meetings. Would you like a short section with country- or region-specific guidance? I have not included one, because such generalisations easily become stereotypes.
3. **Online presence.** Is the online and hybrid guidance in L09 detailed enough, given how many international teaching sessions and webinars are now virtual?
