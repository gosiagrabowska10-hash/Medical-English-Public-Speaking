**What changed in every lesson**

- **Depth.** Teaching content increased from 382–565 words to 1,253–1,432 words, with 4–7 structured sections.
- **Skills, not motivation.** Each lesson now builds a concrete, transferable skill: managing cognitive load and repairing breakdowns (L01); a pre-speaking routine, a scripted opening and a recovery technique (L02); measured scientific confidence (hedging and boosting) and observable goals (L03); thought groups, sentence and contrastive stress, and delivering numbers safely (L07).
- **Three detailed examples per lesson**, each with a realistic setting (congress, grand round, MDT, journal club, teaching), across several specialties. All are labelled in the platform as illustrative and composite.
- **Phrase bank** of 10–11 expressions per lesson, with use, register, pronunciation notes and an example sentence from a real presentation, plus guidance on how to use them in the student's own talks.
- **Exercises** now have step-by-step instructions, a fictional model answer (shown only after the student saves their own response) and self-check criteria.
- **Quizzes** have six questions; three in each lesson are scenario-based and test judgement rather than recall.
- **Assignments** have four transparent assessment criteria for instructors and students.
- **Downloadable handout** (PDF) per lesson, generated from the lesson itself: objectives, key points, evidence notes, common mistakes, phrase bank, exercise worksheet, assignment criteria and references.
- **Common mistakes** now come with the correction ("Instead…").

**Evidence and accuracy**

The platform now distinguishes three kinds of statement, with visible labels:

- **Research evidence** (blue box): a summary of published research, with numbered references at the end of the lesson. 15 references are used across the four lessons. All are published books and peer-reviewed articles from linguistics, psychology and education research. I recommend that the Academic Director checks each reference against the original before launch, as you would for any academic material.
- **Professional recommendation** (gold box): practical guidance from the Academy, explicitly not presented as a research finding. For example, the 130–160 words-per-minute range is labelled as professional guidance, not as an evidence-based threshold.
- **Illustrative examples**: composite and fictional, labelled as such.

Where the evidence is nuanced, the lessons say so. L01 acknowledges that accented speech can be judged less favourably, and L07 explains that fillers are a normal part of speech rather than simply errors. The two unsupported statements from version 1 have been removed. L02 states explicitly that medication for performance anxiety is a clinical decision outside the scope of the programme.

**British English.** All four lessons use British spelling and usage (programme, recognise, practise/practice, anaesthetist, paediatric). "Program" has been changed to "Programme" throughout the public website, the student platform, the legal pages and the course data (68 occurrences), as has "Simulation Center" to "Simulation Centre". Code identifiers are unchanged.
