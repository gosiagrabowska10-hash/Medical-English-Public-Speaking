## 2. Two versions of the workshop

Both versions use the same 2–3-minute extract (opening, key result and close) in all three recording rounds, so that improvements can be compared directly.

### Facilitated workshop: 120 minutes

For cohorts, institutional groups or a live session. Groups of three rotate the roles of speaker, timekeeper and listener.

| # | Component | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Readiness check (completed before the session) | 10 |
| 1 | Briefing | 10 |
| 2 | Round 1 — baseline recording | 15 |
| 3 | Station A — pace, pause, stress, numbers and terms | 20 |
| 4 | Round 2 — re-record and compare | 10 |
| 5 | Station B — projection and vocal variety (L08) | 15 |
| 6 | Station C — presence and recovery (L09) | 15 |
| 7 | Round 3 — performance under pressure | 15 |
| 8 | Debrief and transfer plan | 10 |
| | **Total** | **120** |

### Independent online version: self-paced, about 2¾–3¼ hours

Learners can stop after any component; their progress, recordings and notes are saved. Stations can be repeated as often as they wish. **Recommended rhythm:** three sittings of about an hour, ideally on different days, with Round 3 in the last sitting.

| # | Component | Estimated time | Why it takes longer alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Readiness check | 10–15 min | — |
| 1 | Briefing (video and reading) | 15 min | No facilitator summary |
| 2 | Round 1 — baseline | 20–25 min | Recording set-up; self-measurement; **first take counts**, no retakes |
| 3 | Station A | 25–35 min | Drills repeated until stable; term-stress card checked individually |
| 4 | Round 2 | 15 min | Recording and comparison |
| 5 | Station B | 20–25 min | Warm-up practice and a room-distance recording |
| 6 | Station C | 20–25 min | Video audit without sound |
| 7 | Round 3 — under pressure | 20 min | The pressure card is revealed during the recording (see below) |
| 8 | Debrief, Final Task and transfer plan | 20–25 min | Written analysis completed in the platform |
| | **Total** | **about 165–200 min** | |

**Pressure without a facilitator.** In the independent version the platform reveals a randomly chosen pressure card on screen at a random moment between 45 and 90 seconds into the Round 3 recording. Examples: "You now have one minute left"; "Your slides have failed"; "A participant asks: *Was this statistically significant?*". Until the Speaking Studio is available (Stage 4), learners record on their own device with the card shown on screen. Learners can also ask a colleague to play the facilitator, using the observer sheet in the Workshop Pack.

**The listener comprehension check** (used in criteria 2 and 4). *Facilitated:* the listener writes down the key message and the critical numbers after hearing the extract once. *Independent:* the learner waits at least an hour, plays the recording once at normal speed without pausing, and writes down the key message and numbers, then compares them with the script. A colleague, or later an automatic transcript in the Speaking Studio, can also be used.

**Facilitator guidance** is provided for every component of the facilitated version (purpose, set-up, timing cues, what to observe, common difficulties, feedback language). The independent version has equivalent on-screen instructions and self-check prompts.

## 3. Final Task and assessment criteria (revised)

**Final Task.** Submit the **Round 3 recording** (2–3 minutes, delivered under a pressure card) together with a **written analysis of 250–400 words**. The analysis contains:

- the progress sheet comparing Rounds 1–3,
- two strengths and two priorities,
- a recovery plan,
- a transfer plan for a named upcoming presentation.

**Principles for assessors** (also shown to learners)

- **Intelligibility, not accent.** A non-native accent is never penalised. The question is whether the audience can understand and follow the message without undue effort.
- **Pace serves comprehension.** Words per minute are recorded as *diagnostic information*, never as a pass/fail target. Appropriate pace depends on the content, the room and the audience. Dense data needs more time than a story.
- **Fillers in context.** Fillers are a normal feature of speech. They matter only when their frequency or placement distracts from or obscures the message, for example a cluster in the middle of a key result.
- **Natural over mechanical.** Techniques are rewarded when they serve the audience. Exaggerated pauses, theatrical gestures or forced intonation that make delivery less natural are not rewarded.
- **Audience-centred.** The central question for every criterion is: *does this help this audience understand and trust the message?*

**Levels.** **Competent** is the professional standard for this stage of the programme. **Developing** shows partial or inconsistent control. **Beginning** shows that the skill is not yet established. **To complete the workshop:** Competent in at least five of the six criteria, including the mandatory criteria 2 and 4. Learners who do not reach this level repeat Round 3 with a new pressure card; there is no limit on attempts.

### Criterion 1 — Opening and audience orientation

*Does the audience know quickly why the topic matters, what the speaker will show and how the talk is organised?*

| Beginning | Developing | Competent |
|---|---|---|
| Opens with an apology or with detail (e.g. methods) before any purpose. *Example: "Sorry for my English. So, we included 412 patients aged over 65…"* | Purpose is stated but buried or rushed; the route is missing. *Example: a clear purpose sentence, then straight into data without telling the audience what is coming.* | Context, purpose and route within about 40 seconds, at a pace the audience can absorb, without apology. *Example: "Delirium is often missed after surgery. [pause] Today I'll show you one change in screening that helped us find it earlier — what we changed, what we found, and what we're still unsure about."* |

### Criterion 2 — Pace and pausing for comprehension *(mandatory)*

*Can the audience follow and absorb the content at the pace delivered?*

| Beginning | Developing | Competent |
|---|---|---|
| The key result is delivered so quickly, or so run together with surrounding sentences, that a listener cannot report it correctly after one hearing. Pace increases further under pressure. | The key result is understood, but some passages are rushed or pauses fall inside phrases; under the pressure card pace accelerates noticeably for a period. Or pauses are so long and frequent that delivery becomes mechanical. | A listener reports the key message correctly after one hearing. Pauses fall between thought groups and frame the key result. Pace is adapted to the content (slower for data). Under pressure the speaker reduces content rather than speed. |

### Criterion 3 — Emphasis and intonation

*Does the voice tell the audience what matters and when a point is complete?*

| Beginning | Developing | Competent |
|---|---|---|
| Little or no stress on key words; delivery is flat, or every word is stressed. Findings end with rising "question" intonation. | Key words are stressed in some sentences but not consistently; some conclusions still rise; or variation is exaggerated and sounds artificial. | The main word in each thought group carries the stress; conclusions and results end with a clear fall; rises are used deliberately (questions, unfinished lists). Delivery sounds natural. |

### Criterion 4 — Accuracy and intelligibility of numbers and terminology *(mandatory)*

*Are critical numbers and terms accurate, clear and understood?*

| Beginning | Developing | Competent |
|---|---|---|
| A critical number or term is inaccurate, or is misheard by the listener (e.g. "fifteen" heard as "fifty"); key terms are stressed so that they are hard to recognise. | All numbers are accurate but at least one critical number is not protected (not shown, slowed down or restated); one key term causes hesitation for the listener. | Every critical number is accurate and correctly reported by the listener after one hearing; numbers are protected (shown, slowed down or restated, e.g. "fifteen per cent, roughly one patient in seven"); key terms are stressed so that they are readily recognised. |

### Criterion 5 — Voice and physical presence

*Can the audience hear, see and connect with the speaker?*

| Beginning | Developing | Competent |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence endings are often inaudible; the speaker mostly reads from notes or the screen; distracting movement (swaying, clicker play) dominates. | Generally audible but fades at times; eye contact covers part of the audience only; some purposeful gestures, alongside persistent habits. | Consistently audible to the last word; eye contact includes the whole audience (or the lens online); a stable stance; gestures support the structure; stillness when delivering the key message. |

### Criterion 6 — Composure, recovery and self-analysis

*Does the speaker stay in control of the message, and understand their own performance?*

| Beginning | Developing | Competent |
|---|---|---|
| The disruption derails the talk (long silence, apology, abandoned structure); the analysis is general ("I need more confidence"). | Recovers, but with visible strain or by losing a key point; the analysis notes problems but not their causes. | Acknowledges the disruption briefly, uses a prepared phrase and returns to the key message; the analysis uses evidence from the three recordings, explains causes and sets specific, realistic priorities. |
