### 1. L06 — scientific accuracy of the spoken script (exercise model answer)

**Before:** “We wanted to know whether getting patients with pneumonia out of bed early helps them go home sooner. Here's what we did. We looked back at 412 patients admitted to our hospital over four years. We compared those who got up within the first day with those who did not. What did we find? Patients who mobilised early went home two days sooner — after five days instead of seven. This is the key point: the benefit was clear, and we saw no increase in complications. So what does this mean in practice? We now aim to mobilise every patient with pneumonia on day one.”

**After:** “We wanted to know whether getting patients with pneumonia out of bed early is linked to a shorter hospital stay. Here's what we did. We looked back at the records of 412 patients admitted with community-acquired pneumonia between 2019 and 2023. We compared patients who got up within 24 hours of admission with those who did not. What did we find? Patients who mobilised early had a shorter stay: a median of five days, compared with seven. The difference was statistically significant, with a p-value below 0.01. This is the key point: early mobilisation was associated with a shorter stay. Because we looked back at existing records, this shows an association, not cause and effect.”

The model answer now also states what did not change: “the study design (retrospective cohort), the type of result (an association) and the reported figures (median 5 vs 7 days, p<0.01). No finding or recommendation was added.” Two further inaccuracies were also corrected: “over four years” (the abstract says 2019–2023) and “within the first day” (the abstract says within 24 hours).
